Okutama is the mountainous western edge of Tokyo, but a car is not essential for a day focused on walking. JR Okutama Station is the practical hub: from this area, you can choose between a short nature-focused visit close to the station, a railway-linked hiking course that finishes in Hikawa, or a longer historic-road walk that requires more deliberate return planning.

These are not like-for-like routes. The available official information is uneven, and that matters when you are deciding how much of the day to commit. One option has a documented distance but no confirmed duration; another has a duration but no confirmed distance; the longest has both, plus a bus-dependent return. Rather than trying to rank them by an invented difficulty scale, it is more useful to choose by time, interests and how simple you want the journey home to be.

## At a glance: three different ways to walk in Okutama

| Walking choice | Start and finish | Documented distance and time | Main character | Return considerations |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Hikawa Valley promenade or Toke Trail | Okutama Station/Hikawa area | Toke Trail: about 1.3 km; duration not confirmed. Hikawa Valley: distance and duration not confirmed. | Forest, river and valley scenery | Station-area possibilities; no bus is required simply to access the area. |
| Otama Walking Trail | Kori Station (古里駅) to the Hikawa/Okutama Station area | About 2 hours 30 minutes; total distance not confirmed. | A town-designated hiking course with nature, river/valley and settlement scenery | A railway-linked, station-to-station structure; the walk finishes in the Okutama Station area. |
| Okutama Mukashi Michi | Okutama Station to Lake Okutama | About 10 km; about 4 hours. | Historic-road context, villages, river scenery and cultural features | Plan around the current bus timetable from Lake Okutama. |

The “best for” guidance below is editorial advice, not an official ranking. Conditions, especially after weather or maintenance work, need checking close to your travel date.

## 1. Limited time: the Hikawa Valley promenade and Toke Trail area

For a shorter visit centred on forest, river and valley scenery near Okutama Station, look first at the **Hikawa Valley promenade** and the **Toke Trail**. Think of these as two nearby station-area walking possibilities, not as one officially documented combined route.

The distinction is worth keeping clear. Okutama Town identifies the Hikawa Valley promenade separately from the Toke Trail, which is described as a dedicated forest-therapy route. The Toke Trail is approximately **1.3 km**, while a current official walking duration has not been confirmed. For the Hikawa Valley promenade, the verified material does not state a distance or a duration. That means it would be misleading to combine the two into a single route total or promise a particular length of outing.

For independent travelers, this is nevertheless the sensible category to consider when the priority is a station-area nature visit rather than a half-day hike. You can decide on the day how much time you have for the riverside and forest setting, without building the whole plan around a distant endpoint or a return bus.

There is an important current-conditions caveat. Okutama Town announced that a previously closed section of the Hikawa Valley promenade reopened on April 29, 2023, but that notice is not a guarantee that every section is currently passable or unaffected by heavy rain. Before setting out, check the town’s [current Hikawa Valley promenade notice](https://www.town.okutama.tokyo.jp/1/kankosangyoka/kankojoho/3/2718.html) and its latest visitor information. The town’s [walking material](https://www.town.okutama.tokyo.jp/material/files/group/8/20250401pamphlet.pdf) is also a useful starting point for identifying the separate valley and Toke Trail options.

**Choose this type of outing if:** you have limited time, want greenery and water close to the station area, and do not need a single long through-walk with a fixed endpoint.

**Do not assume:** a combined distance, a combined duration, a formal difficulty grade, specific facilities, or exact links between the two walks. Those details were not confirmed in the available official information.

## 2. A longer railway-linked walk: Otama Walking Trail

The **Otama Walking Trail** is the middle-ground choice for travelers who want a more substantial walk but prefer a railway-based structure over a bus return. It runs from **Kori Station (古里駅)** to the Hikawa/Okutama Station area and is categorized by Okutama Town as a hiking course.

Its documented duration is approximately **2 hours 30 minutes**. Official material available for this comparison does not confirm a total distance, so it is better to plan around the time figure than to turn it into a guessed kilometre count.

The practical appeal is the direction of travel. You use JR to reach the Kori starting area, then walk towards Hikawa and Okutama Station. At the end, you are back in the station area rather than at a lakeside endpoint dependent on a scheduled bus. That does not make the trail officially “easier” or “more convenient” in every respect—there is no verified standardized difficulty rating, elevation figure or route-specific facilities list—but it does make the return structure relatively straightforward for a traveler organizing the day around rail.

This is also not simply a station-area stroll. Okutama Town classifies it as a hiking course, and its official course page and map should be checked before travel for the current route information. The course combines nature, river or valley scenery and settlement landscapes rather than focusing on the historical-road experience of Mukashi Michi.

**Choose this type of outing if:** you can allow roughly two and a half hours for the walk itself, want a genuine hiking-course option, and would rather finish in the Okutama Station/Hikawa area than plan a return bus from Lake Okutama.

**Plan carefully:** confirm the current [Otama Walking Trail course information](https://www.town.okutama.tokyo.jp/1/kankosangyoka/kankojoho/4/918.html) and town notices before leaving. The accessible official information does not confirm the total distance, intermediate exit points, toilets or a formal shorter version, so avoid treating a map from a previous trip or an old online account as proof of present conditions.

## 3. For history and a fuller day: Okutama Mukashi Michi

Choose **Okutama Mukashi Michi** if the historical landscape is your main reason for walking. Officially described from **Okutama Station to Lake Okutama**, it is approximately **10 km** and takes approximately **four hours**. That is a meaningful time and distance commitment, not a casual stroll to fit into a spare hour.

The route follows the context of the old Ome Kaido. This is the walk’s defining character: historic-road scenery sits alongside village landscapes, the Tama River area, bridges, shrines and stone features. It offers a different kind of day from the shorter forest-and-valley possibilities near Hikawa. Still, do not interpret “old Ome Kaido” to mean that every part of the present walking route is identical to the historic road itself.

The trade-off is return planning. The documented walking direction is Okutama Station to Lake Okutama. Nishi Tokyo Bus serves Lake Okutama from Okutama Station, so a bus can support a return from the endpoint, but the useful service for your day depends on the current timetable. Check the [Nishi Tokyo Bus website](https://www.nisitokyobus.co.jp/) before departure rather than assuming that a service shown on an older map or blog will suit your finish time.

Mukashi Michi also has the strongest need for a current-condition check. The official route information does not provide a current closure statement, while older official records document risks including rockfall and fallen trees. There is no verified standardized difficulty label or elevation-gain figure, and no confirmed intermediate exits or defined shortened version in the information available here. Those gaps are a reason to prepare conservatively, not to fill them with assumptions.

**Choose this type of outing if:** you have a larger block of daylight, are drawn to historical and cultural landscape as much as nature, and are comfortable planning around a current bus timetable.

**Before committing:** read Okutama Town’s [Mukashi Michi route information](https://www.town.okutama.tokyo.jp/gyosei/6/kankojoho/miru/1721.html), look for current notices, and confirm the bus plan. The published 10 km and four-hour figures apply to the described Okutama Station–Lake Okutama route, not just selected sightseeing stops along it.

## How to make the choice

If your day is constrained by time, or you simply want to keep plans loose, start with the Hikawa Valley promenade and Toke Trail area. Their station-area setting is the key advantage, although the official evidence does not support a precise combined itinerary.

If you want a walk with a clearer half-day time commitment and a rail-oriented finish, Otama Walking Trail is the useful contrast. Its approximately two-and-a-half-hour duration gives you a planning anchor, while the absence of a verified distance is an honest limitation to bear in mind.

If you want the strongest historical story and are prepared for roughly 10 km and four hours of walking, choose Mukashi Michi. Build in enough time to reach Lake Okutama and make a current, timetable-based decision about the bus back.

In all cases, do not confuse a documented route title or an old course map with a guarantee of present-day passability. Recheck Okutama Town notices for closures, maintenance and weather-related issues shortly before travel. For Mukashi Michi, add a check of the current Nishi Tokyo Bus timetable.

## Optional post-walk stop: VERTERE near the station

If your walk ends in the Hikawa/Okutama Station area on a weekend or holiday, a local craft-beer stop may fit naturally into the end of the day. **VERTERE Okutama Taproom** is at Hikawa 212. Its official listing gives hours of **11:00–19:30 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays**. Seating is first-come, first-served, and reservations are not accepted.

VERTERE also lists a **Station Bottle Shop** at Hikawa 211, with listed hours of **10:00–18:00 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays** and closure Monday through Friday. This can be a practical alternative if you want to take something away rather than build the rest of the day around a seated stop.

Opening arrangements can change, including occasional weekday operations at the Taproom, so check VERTERE’s [official shop information](https://verterebrew.com/shoplist/) before you travel. Do not assume a brewery tour, production-facility access or a particular language service: those details are not established by the available official listing.

## A final pre-departure checklist

- Check Okutama Town notices and current route pages for weather damage, closures or maintenance.
- For Hikawa Valley, confirm current conditions rather than relying on the earlier reopening announcement.
- For Otama Walking Trail, review the current course map and do not estimate the missing distance.
- For Mukashi Michi, check both route conditions and the current Nishi Tokyo Bus timetable before starting from Okutama Station.
- If VERTERE is part of the plan, verify that day’s opening information on its official site.

The useful lesson is that walking from Okutama can mean three very different outings: a short station-area encounter with forest and water, a roughly two-and-a-half-hour railway-linked hiking course, or a roughly 10 km historic-road walk whose return needs bus planning. Choosing between them is less about finding a single “best” trail than matching the walk to the time, interest and transport certainty you actually have.
